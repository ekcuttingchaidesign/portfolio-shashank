The batter didn’t bat like a cricketer

The game’s batter is a 3D rig built entirely in code: every stroke is a handful of poses on a millisecond clock. Nobody on the project is an animator, so strokes were guessed.

  1. The bat turned in his hands like a fanAfter the ball, the blade kept spinning between his wrists.
  2. The wrong side of the blade met the ballThe curved back or the edge, where every real stroke uses the flat face.
  3. Follow-throughs went through his own helmetOn the way round, the bat passed straight through the body.

One stroke at a time, same eight steps

I bring the reference and the eye. Claude Code studies, poses, publishes and tests. Every stroke went round the same loop.

  1. MeSend a clip

    A slow-motion net session, a coaching film or a broadcast replay. Phone screen recordings are fine.

  2. ClaudeStudy it frame by frame

    Pulls stills every 80–250 ms and reads the feet, the hands and the bat at each one.

  3. ClaudeSay what it sees

    Before touching any code, it writes back the footwork, contact point, face of the bat and finish. I correct it.

  4. ClaudePose the rig

    Key poses on the stroke’s clock: load, contact, through, finish, hold, home.

  5. ClaudePublish a preview

    A live page with the stroke on a scrubber, 0.1× speed and five cameras.

  6. MeReview at a timestamp

    Scrub to the moment it’s wrong, screenshot it, say it in cricket words.

  7. ClaudeFix it and add a test

    Every rejected behaviour becomes a check, so it can’t come back.

  8. Me“Open the PR and merge it”

    Merged into production, the branch reset, on to the next stroke.

Steps 4 to 7 repeat until it looks right: usually two or three rounds.

Three words fixed half the bugs

Most of my feedback was about which side of the bat faced where. Once we used the same three names, a one-line note was enough.

Flat faceMeets the ball in every stroke. At a wrap it looks at the sky.
Curved backRests on the shoulder at a wrap and faces the helmet at a high finish.
EdgeNever on the shoulder and never on the ball, unless it’s an edge to slip.
FanMy word for the bat spinning in the hands after impact. Banned everywhere.

Five strokes, round by round

My actual feedback, word for word, and what changed after it.

Cut off the spinner

New stroke, from my own net video2 roundsPR #55
Brief

“A ball pitching outside off turns in, so the batsman makes room by moving towards leg, then cuts. It’s played on the back foot. Analyse carefully the feet, the follow-through, the hands, and how the bat lands on the opposite shoulder.”

Round 2

“The bat will not go over the head, it will land on the opposite shoulder. The hands going over the head look forceful. Like the hook, hold a little when the bat lands on the shoulder.”

The rules

“It should not be out every time. It could be a dot off the inside edge, or a single if it’s close to good timing. … Even with poor contact it should go towards off.”

Bat over the head→Hands at the brow, bat onto the shoulder→Held, like the hook
  • Room made, not givenBack and away to leg, front foot drawn back, stance opened wide.
  • No forced finishHands stop at the peak of the helmet; the bat tips past the ear onto the shoulder and holds.
  • Fair at the edgesA ball going on down leg gets a cramped cut: squeezed to point or off the pad, out only when misread and mistimed.

Square cut

Rebuilt from a net clip3 roundsPR #69
Round 2

“Same mistakes again. Curved side of the bat hits the ball, but flat face of the bat hits the ball in every bat. The hands will be stretched, since it’s played away from the body.”

Original→Full rebuild→Original + 3 targeted fixes
The lesson

The full rebuild was worse than the original. Keeping what worked and fixing three named things got approved.

  • Flat face to the bowlerThe forearms turn it over the ball on the way down.
  • Arms out straightHe leaves 30 cm of room and leans away. Elbows went from 26° to 74°.
  • Back of the blade on the shoulderThe bottom hand rolls over at the wrap.

Cover drive

One stroke, three versionsSeveral roundsPR #53
V1 40%V2V3 40%

How often each version is drawn, after the swipe is judged.

Brief

“Watch the video and analyse how the batsman plays the cover drive. It has the same shot from two angles in slo-mo. Figure out what improvement we need in our rig.”

Feedback

“The bat should return along its path to the batting stance. Currently it rotates twice in the hand.”

Next ask

“Create a v3 that meets the ball beside the front pad. I want to preview and experience it first.”

Decision

“Let’s keep all 3 versions. V1 40%, V2 20%, V3 40%. … V3 and V2 should result in 4 only.”

  • Variety without luckThe version is drawn after the swipe is judged, so nobody can time for it. A rival on the same seed sees the same version.
  • V2 and V3 stay downMiddled is four, never six. Skied and not caught still runs away for four.
  • Home along its own pathThe bat goes back down its swing into the stance and never turns in his hands.

A held wrap3 roundsPR #80
Round 1

“Around 669 ms, when the bat is on the opposite shoulder, the side blade lands on the shoulder instead of the curved face.”

Round 2

“No, the side face is still on the shoulder. Flat face towards the sky and curved face on the shoulder.”

Round 3

“Just hold the pose a little at 672 ms and then return to stance smoothly.”

Edge on shoulder→Wrong metric→Face to sky→Hold + smooth home
  • Round 1 measured the wrong thingThe check was “face away from the shoulder”. My eye said otherwise, so the test became “face to the sky”.
  • A held finish220 ms of stillness at the wrap, then 380 ms home.
  • Smooth, not hurriedHome keys spaced by how far the hands travel, eased in and out.

Flick, version 2

One screenshot, one round1 roundPR #82
My screenshot

“The follow-through will end with the curved face of the bat towards the helmet and the flat face towards the bowler’s end. Currently the bat’s side edge is towards the helmet.”

The fix: the wrists’ roll keeps going the same way as the bat rises, about 100° more, and stops when the face looks at the bowler. One screenshot with a timestamp, one round.

Tests hold the taste

The rig has nine invisible spheres: helmet, shoulders, elbows, hips and knees. Every stroke is swept every 4 ms to check the blade never enters one. A preview shows what looks wrong. The tests catch what I’d never spot at full speed.

  • 308 msBlade through the back elbowFlick v3, as the bat tipped over. Fixed by carrying the hands further out in front.
  • 100 msBack shoulder, then the front hipTurning the face to square leg swung the blade broadside into the body on the way down. Fixed by leaving the contact pose alone and turning the face separately.
  • < 1 mmToo close to a forearmA near miss on the pick-up. A 2 cm change to one pose cleared it.
  • Test bugThe checker was wrong onceA helper sent every “version 3” ball to the cover drive’s contact point. Fixed the test, not the stroke.
  • < 2°The fan, banned by number“No wrist roll” became a rule: the bat may barely turn in his hands after the ball.

How to direct an AI on motion

Ask it to explain first

Make it describe the clip before it writes code. Half the corrections happen there, and they cost nothing.

Analyse the video and first tell me what you understand.

Point at a millisecond

A screenshot with a timecode beats any description of motion.

Around 669 ms the side blade lands on the shoulder.

Agree on names

Flat face, curved back, edge, fan. Shared words made every later note one line long.

Flat face to the sky, curved face on the shoulder.

Scope the change

Say what must not move. It protects the parts you already approved.

Only change. The follow-through is fine, don’t change that.

Reject rebuilds early

When a redo is worse, say so and go back. Targeted fixes on the original won every time.

The current version looks way better than your new version.

Turn taste into tests

Each “never do that again” became an automated check. That’s why later rounds didn’t break earlier strokes.
