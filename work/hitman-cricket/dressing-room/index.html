Players came back. Nothing to chase.

Before this update I read a month of analytics. Retention was strong: of the players who came back on a later day, three in four came back the very next day.

What was working

76%

of returning players came back the very next day.

  • Next day266
  • 2–7 days later73
  • 8–30 days later11

What was missing

69%

of the 57 innings started each day were The Blast: the same five overs.

  • The Blast69%
  • Every other mode31%

Playing more changed nothing. There was nothing to earn and nothing to unlock, and the only thing to chase was a leaderboard most players would never top.

but

Daily players had nothing to work for. Give them a reason to play one more innings.

Play. Earn. Wear.

11 Oct. It started with one question to Claude: can a batter carry stickers on his bat, the way Rohit plays with CEAT and Kohli with MRF? That grew into a room where your whole look is earned.

  1. Every innings countsIn every mode, towards a kit or a bat. The innings you played before the room existed count too.

  2. The item waits for youHit a target and it’s there in the Dressing Room, with an unlock moment of its own.

  3. You walk out in itIn every mode. In Rival Matches your opponent sees it too.

“Kits are free but you have to unlock them. A new player must earn them by scoring runs or playing matches. We need to design it like an experience.”

My brief to Claude, 11 Oct
Earned, never bought

No store and no prices. Every item comes from playing.

History counts

A loyal player opens the room to find things already waiting for them.

You press unlock

No pop-ups in the middle of a flow. The moment plays when the player asks for it.

Every lock says what it takes

Try a locked item on, see the exact target, and go and play for it in one tap.

Drawn flat in Figma, wrapped onto 3D

Day one. I wanted to design bat stickers the way I design anything else: flat, in Figma. So we built a template from real bat sizes, and the game wraps whatever I draw onto the 3D blade.

  1. Measure

    Real SG and SS batsI sent photos. Claude measured them in pixels and reshaped the 3D blade to match.

  2. Template

    Four panels in FigmaFace, edge, back, edge. The willow and guides sit on locked layers, so an export holds only the stickers.

  3. Pack

    One sheet a batA script packs the four exports into one 720 × 1400 image the game reads.

  4. Wrap

    Onto the bladeEach panel lands on its own side of the bat, over the willow.

Flat failed. 3D won.

Day one. The first room was drawn flat: a batter in an open locker, jerseys on hangers, bats on a wooden table, sheets of progress bars. I turned it down.

“These screens won’t look like a professional game until we show some 3D here. A slow-turning platform with the batter on it, that I can rotate too, with a spotlight above him.”

My feedback on the flat version

Claude rebuilt it as a working prototype the same evening, with the game’s real batter, my real bat art and the real kit colours, so what I reviewed was what would ship. “This is a massive improvement” was my next message.

Five rounds to make a target easy to read

Locked items needed to say what they take. My reaction to each version was the same: too busy.

Round 1

Chips for every requirement

Round 2

Several progress bars stacked. “Still cluttered, significant cognitive load.”

Round 3

A “3 / 13” count and Easy, Medium, Hard tags. “Give it breathing space.”

Round 4

All tiles squeezed into one row. “Earlier was better.”

Shipped

A plain checklist with ticks, and one big figure like 212 / 250 when there’s one goal.

What broke on my phone

Day two. Every change went to a preview link. I tested it on my phone, circled what was wrong, and sent the screenshot back. These are those screenshots.

Before

The camera jumped on every tap

Problem. The text under each kit was a different height, so the stage shrank and grew and the batter zoomed with it.

Fix. A fixed height for the info panel, and one camera for every kit.

Before

Everything sat on top of the hero

Problem. The prototype was drawn for an 874 px tall phone. In Safari, with its bars, I got about 660 px, and the stage was squeezed to 180 px.

Fix. The shelf moved from under the batter to a rail down the right. The pedestal gets the screen’s full height, and the camera is never closer than the prototype.

Before and after

A rail that shows there’s more

Problem. Exactly four tiles fitted, and nothing said to scroll.

Fix. The rail is sized to stop partway through a tile, so the next one peeks out.

My drawing, then the game

The room needed a place

I drew a locker corridor and asked to see it before it went in. Claude showed four treatments side by side; I picked “light and soft”, asked for the far end to fade, and shrank the ceiling lights that ran into the title. A script bakes my drawing into one 11 KB image, so I can redraw it and run the script again.

Before and after

Noise only a phone could show

Problem. The glow looked clean on a laptop, but phone GPUs broke it into black speckles.

Fix. The glow textures were rebuilt in a way phone GPUs draw cleanly.

An unlock you’d want to show someone

Day two. The first unlock just faded the item in. I called it “boring and slow” and sent CRED Garage as the reference: joyful, confetti, the bat going full screen as its stickers appear.

Boom is free, but you unlock it yourself

Every player gets the Boom bat. My first build handed it over the moment you walked in, and that took the best part away.

“Let the user enter the room and explore. A small red dot on the Bat tab, a nudge on the item, and they tap the button to unlock. Then the animation comes.”

My feedback, 12 Oct

After launch I saw the same gap for loyal players: my own record had already earned Meg, Ice Cold and Roar, and they were simply there, with no moment. So everything earned before the room existed now waits too, each with its own UNLOCK key.

What you earn is the hero, and it’s live

Most games show rewards as small icons in a grid. Here the item you pick is the biggest thing on the screen. Drag the pedestal to spin your batter, try on a kit you haven’t earned yet, and watch him play a shot in it.

The batter is the game’s own 3D model, so the room shows exactly what you’ll see out in the middle.

Fair to veterans

Only one player had reached Hitman in The Blast before launch. History counts for everything, but the top two bats also ask for a quarter of the target again, made after launch, so that player still has to earn them.

Re-tuned a week later, from the numbers

Survival is a fifth of all innings, and players win about one in eight. A target of 50 Survival wins was out of everyone’s reach. The Blast is two thirds of what gets played, so Electric moved there.

A launch story on the live stage

Day three. An update nobody sees doesn’t exist, and the game announces updates as stories. I made a Lottie for this one and it came out at 1.7 MB — a lot to download on mobile data before you can bat.

Play the story on the room’s own stage

The stories use the same 3D stage as the Dressing Room. SUIT UP turns through every kit on the batter; EARN YOUR WILLOW turns through every bat. A kit or a bat added later appears in the stories by itself, with no new animation to make.

1.7 MBthe Lottie I would have shipped
~8 KBof compressed code, reusing the room

Read these numbers honestly. This is a small game, and GoatCounter counts events, not unique people. Player profiles and new shots shipped in the same two weeks, and new visitors fell by about two thirds after an early-October spike, so the rise in long sessions came from returning players — who the room was made for. The jump is clear; how much of it is the room alone, this data can’t say.

Four things I’d do again

Prototype in the real medium

Flat mock-ups of a 3D room hid the problem. One working 3D prototype, with the real model and the real art, settled the direction in an evening.

Design for the smallest real screen

The prototype was right on an 874 px phone and broke at 660 px in Safari. Testing on my own phone after every change caught it on day two, not after launch.

Let players set off the delight

The same animation feels better when you press the key yourself. Waiting items with a dot and an UNLOCK key beat a pop-up every time.

Track for the story you’ll tell later

Every innings now records its kit, its bat, its day and hour, and the day each item was won. In December that becomes each player’s year in review.
