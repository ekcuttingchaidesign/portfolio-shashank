Fifty strangers don’t make you bat again

Hitman Cricket keeps a top-50 board for every mode and a career for every named player. The boards worked as a scoreboard and failed as a reason to play. A name on a board was text. You couldn’t learn anything about the person, compare yourself to them, or do anything about them.

Three hypotheses, because we hadn’t measured them

  1. The boards had no next step. You looked at them and left.
  2. Players drifted after a few innings. Nothing told them something had happened while they were away.
  3. “Get on the board” is a weak goal. “Beat Kabir’s 187” has a face on it.

“What if we can build a profile card for each player. You tap anyone on the leaderboard, you see their profile, their stats and all. You can challenge them for a Rival Match.”

My first message to Claude

Discuss first, then draw

I worked with Claude Code inside the game’s repository, which changed what a brainstorm could be. When I asked what a profile could show, the answer came from the data the game actually stores. Ideas arrived already priced and already possible. Nothing was drawn until I’d made the call.

  1. Brainstorm against the codeWhat exists, what each idea needs, what it would break.
  2. Price itI pasted our database usage screen and asked whether this could stay free.
  3. Wireframe in low fidelityAnnotated with numbered pins and a motion spec, then critique and cut. Four rounds.
  4. Design it in FigmaOn the game’s kit: section 14, four lanes, with the motion notes carried over.
  5. Build, play it on a preview, fixAnd count every step on GoatCounter from the first day.

The walls I designed against

Cost. A free database tier

The game runs on Upstash Redis. When we started it had used 143K of its 500K free monthly commands, about 12K a day. Every feature had to be designed in commands, the way you’d design in pixels.

Trust. Security came first

While mapping what a profile could show, Claude noticed that every board row was sending the internal player id the server trusts as proof of who is writing. The night before profiles went live, the boards switched to a one-way handle. Profiles open by that handle.

Play. The scoreboard is sacred

The in-game scoreboard stays exactly as it is: same fields, layout, look and motion. Anything a chase needs lives beside it, never in it.

Audience. Many players are young

Anything a player writes for others to read must never be usable as a way to contact someone.

In the wireframes 18 Oct

1Out first

Challenge them to a Rival Match

My original idea. A Rival Match is played by link, so “challenge” on a stranger’s profile meant inventing an inbox, spam limits, a decline flow and an opt-out. And most of the fifty players on any board aren’t playing on any given day.

Why it wentIt depends on the other player turning up, and needs an inbox before it works at all.
What replaced itChase their best. A normal innings with their best as the target. Works against every row on day one and costs nothing on the server.
2Out on a detail

“12 runs to pass Dhruv”

The innings boards rank your best single innings. If his best is 187 and yours is 175, 12 more runs doesn’t pass him. You need one innings of 188. Only the career ladders add up.

Why it wentThe copy promised arithmetic the board doesn’t do.
What replaced it“Beat 187” for a best innings, which also covers ties the tiebreak decides. “12 runs to pass” only for a career total.
3Drawn, then cut

The nemesis on the cover

The first wireframes put your nemesis on the cover: a line in the board widget and, on a return visit, an orange “Meera passed you” panel with its own key. I cut it once I saw it drawn. The cover is the front door to every mode, and a rival’s face on it every visit turns the welcome into a nag.

Why it wentA rival’s face and a second orange key competed with PLAY.
What replaced itActivity moved to the player’s own profile, where looking at your rivals is a choice.
4Out on privacy

“Kabir and Meera viewed your profile”

Named visitors are the strongest curiosity hook a profile can have, and they cost the most trust. If looking tells them you looked, people stop looking, and the profiles stop doing their job.

Why it wentIt punishes the behaviour the feature exists to encourage.
What replaced itA rule: looking is private, acting is public. Views are a count. A chase or a pass carries your name, like a score on a board.
5Out to reuse

A new widget for activity

Something still had to say “something happened” from the cover. The cover already had an activity widget turning over your best, the leader and a big recent score, so new activity became another line in it.

Why it wentA second container for the same kind of news makes the cover louder and teaches nothing.
What replaced itNew line types in the existing widget, new ones first on a visit, each opening My profile.
6Out in review

CHASE BACK on every event

If Meera chased your 187 and fell 6 short, you’re still ahead of her. A key to chase her lower score means nothing.

Why it wentA key on every row means the key means nothing.
What replaced itWIN IT BACK only where they’re ahead of you. A row you’ve settled dims and says “You won it back”.

In Figma 18–19 Oct · section 14

7Four tries

Career cards, poster tabs, segmented tabs

My profile needed a way to switch between the three games. I tried three options on one board first: career cards (a small card per game with your rung and figure), poster tabs (the mode posters cut down), and quiet tabs (words and a small badge, the pick underlined in its colour). Then segmented tabs as a fourth.

Why it wentUnder a hero already themed to your rung, posters and cards were a second loud thing. The segmented control looked like a settings screen.
What replaced itQuiet tabs: the calmest, and they keep the eye on the face. They say least about the other two games, which is fine on a page about you.
8Out on tone

“Meera passed you” as the headline

The wireframes led My profile with who took your place. In Figma that read as a grudge with a name on it. The headline became Rank down: about you, the line your place fell across, and what happened, without the name. Who did it stays one tap down in the activity sheet, with WIN IT BACK. It stands for two days at most, and playing the WIN IT BACK innings takes it down sooner.

Why it wentLeading with another player’s name made the first thing you see about someone else.
What replaced it“Rank down · You’re #5 now · Someone beat your 187 in the Blast.”

In the build 19 Oct · first play on the preview

9Out on the rules

Chasing their runs in Test Survival

Survival isn’t ranked on runs. A win ranks on fewest balls, a draw on most runs, a loss on how long it lasted. Chasing someone’s runs there chased the wrong thing. The chase follows what they did: win faster than their win, outscore their draw, or outlast their loss.

Why it wentThe target didn’t match the board it came from.
What replaced itOUTLAST KABIR, “41 balls to outlast”, “Survive 13 more”, “You outlasted Kabir by 6 balls”.
10Out on the phone

The chip that stayed, and names that didn’t fit

The wireframe kept the target chip on screen after you’d passed them, reading “Passed Kabir”. In play it was clutter once the job was done, so the chip now leaves and the passed moment takes its place. And a name runs to fourteen letters, which ran CHASE …’S 216 off the key.

Why it wentBoth only showed up on a real phone, with real names.
What replaced itThe chip goes once passed. Every line that names them has a short form (CHASE THEIR 216), swapped in only where the name doesn’t fit.

My profile and the bio

Designed to stay free

The free tier allows 500K Redis commands a month, and we were on course for about 310K in October. A profile that fetched everything on every tap would have spent a third of that at a thousand opens a day, so I used what the phone already had.

What this can and can’t say. Profiles were used, and that’s direct: 31% of board opens became a profile, against a target of 20%, and 30% of those became a chase. These events only exist because the feature does.

The game-wide shifts can’t be credited to profiles. The same nine days had the flick’s new versions, the end of the new strokes’ launch week, Super10’s “coming soon” card, new link previews and the walk-on. There’s no control group, and 56 profile opens is too few to move a game-wide average on their own. Test Marathon starts fell from about 15 a day to about 8 from 23 October, which doesn’t line up with the profile events. GoatCounter counts visitors, not every tap, and launch evening includes my own testing.

Designing with an AI in the room

It made the brainstorm honest

Because Claude was reading the code, every idea came back with what it would touch and what it would cost. The security gap only surfaced because the conversation went from “what could a profile show” to “where does that data come from”.

Cheap drawing made cutting cheap

Fifteen annotated frames in one session meant I could see the nemesis on the cover and reject it the same afternoon. When drawing is slow, you defend what you drew.

The judgement stayed mine

The first plan put the strongest hook on the cover and named the people who viewed you. Both were defensible and both were wrong for this game. The constraints that shaped the result were mine; the AI’s value was writing them onto every frame once I’d said them.

What happens now

  1. Steer chases toward winnable targetsThe player just above you, shown on every profile and the intro.
  2. Make a lost chase that sets a new best feel like progressOn the end card and on My profile.
  3. Run the server-side reportAnd add its looks, beats and passes to this page.
  4. Find the bio its momentAnd measure the fill rate again after 30 days.
